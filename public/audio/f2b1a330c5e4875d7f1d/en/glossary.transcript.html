7. Glossary

Chapter 7: Glossary

This glossary explains key Toolbox terms in alphabetical order. It helps you read chapters 1–6 without prior knowledge.

Agenda setting. A tactic for making an issue publicly visible, for example through an action, a petition, or a striking image for the media.

Framing. Naming and telling a problem and its solution so that they are understood in a new way.

Kiezblock. The Berlin variant of the superblock, strongly driven by local initiatives.

Low-traffic neighbourhood. A form of traffic calming that reduces through traffic, discussed as a Viennese variant called Low-Traffic-Grätzl.

Milestone. A visible intermediate goal that shows a campaign is making progress and keeps it on track.

Mobilisation. Work through which people understand why they participate and become ready to act together.

Modal filter. A barrier such as a bollard, planter ring or concrete element that stops through traffic but lets access and delivery traffic pass.

Monitoring. Systematic observation and documentation of traffic, use, support and political progress.

Narrative. The story an initiative tells about its goal, window of opportunity, alliances, and intended change.

Street design. Jointly designing streets with simple tools such as a street plan, banner, or tree visualisation.

Superblock. An area where through traffic is systematically interrupted so streets inside can be used more safely and in more diverse ways.

Tactical urbanism. Temporary, low-cost interventions in urban space that test changes and make them visible.

Theory of change. An assumption about how social and political change happens, helping you choose tactics deliberately.

Through traffic. Traffic that only crosses an area without having a destination or origin there.

Window of opportunity. A favourable moment for change, such as an election, media attention or a political decision.

Leiwande Straßen Radar. A tool for assessing quality of life, safety, and where action is needed.

Street Shit Bingo. A tool for a guided walk that helps participants spot obstacles, hazards, and surprising uses of street space.

Street Design Table. A design tool for adapting a street plan with paper pieces to explore alternative uses of street space.

Tree Love Set. A set of tree-shaped costumes to show where shade and greenery are needed.

Supergrätzl. Viennese term for a superblock, literally a 'super neighbourhood'.

Play street. A street temporarily closed to traffic so children can play.

Quality of stay. An indicator describing how comfortable and usable a public space is for people spending time there.

Places to spend time. Public spaces where people can pause, sit, meet, or take part in activities.

Plan to win. An initiative's idea of how change will happen and which actions will help achieve it.

LTN. Abbreviation for low-traffic neighbourhood, a neighbourhood traffic-filtering approach.

Traffic evaporation. When road space for cars is reduced, overall traffic often decreases instead of simply moving elsewhere.

Hot spot mapping. Collecting people's experiences of specific places on a shared map to reveal patterns, for example heat islands or dangerous crossings.

Public Life Data Protocol. An open standard by the Gehl Institute for collecting comparable data on how people move through and stay in public spaces.
