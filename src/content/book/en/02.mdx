# 2. The power of superblocks

Superblocks reduce through traffic and create space for spending time, walking, cycling, greenery and neighbourhood life. Barcelona made the concept internationally known; Berlin developed Kiezblocks, a version strongly driven by local initiatives; Vienna has a high-quality implementation in Supergrätzl Favoriten and is now discussing cheaper "low-traffic neighbourhoods". Studies prove the benefits for residents. With a wide range of design options, superblocks are feasible in most neighbourhoods and across varying budgets.

## What changes

A superblock is more than a single piece of street furniture or a greening project. The central lever is traffic management:

- Modal filters, one-way systems, bollards and other measures prevent through traffic.
- Access and delivery traffic remain possible, but move more slowly and under more direct control.
- Space inside the area becomes available for spending time, routes to school, small open spaces, trees, seating and safe junctions.

<TableWrap>

| Question | Superblock logic | Single measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traffic | Through traffic is systematically interrupted across the area | One junction, pavement or section is improved |
| Space gained | Several streets become newly usable together | One specific place becomes safer or more pleasant |
| Participation | Neighbourhood, schools, businesses and district need a shared vision | Affected groups can be involved in a targeted way |

</TableWrap>

## Learning from Berlin

The Kiezblock initiatives from Berlin show that you don’t need perfect plans from the start — in fact, it is all about a handful of residents starting a bottom-up initiative that brings together neighbours. What matters is connecting to local allies and building power from below.

<PullQuote>
  Do not wait for the finished master plan: a clear area, documented evidence of the problem, and first local allies are enough to start.
</PullQuote>

## Starting your initiative

[To be embedded] Valentina Haas of Changing Cities explains how to start a superblock initiative

### Superblock in seven steps

1. Define the area: Choose a neighbourhood where you can clearly identify issues with through traffic, routes to school, heat, missing trees or unsafe junctions. You or at least most of your group, should be residents of the neighbourhood.
2. Document the problem: Record observations, photos, points of conflict and, where possible, traffic counts.
3. Form a core team: Clarify roles for research, neighbourhood contact, social media, district contacts and actions.
4. Define a simple key goal: For example, "no through traffic around the school" or "safe routes and more space to spend time inside the neighbourhood".
<MascotBox title="I have an idea!" pose="point" animated lang="en">
  Positive slogans are most effective because they spark imagination: "Safer streets for children! More shade in the summer! Spaces to rest and connect!"
</MascotBox>
5. Make a first sketch: Work with a base map and consider where modal filters, one-way streets or places to spend time might fit.
6. Build alliances: Talk to residents, parents, schools, local associations, shops and district politicians.
7. Make your demand public: Use actions, media work, petitions, district motions or open letters, depending on the political opportunity available.

## Playful interventions

Playful interventions show how differently a street could be used. They are a perfect tool, e.g. for superblock initiatives, to attract public attention and connect to your community. They can be easy to join: chalk, mobile seating, pop-up games, temporary greenery, information stalls, street picnics or visualisations using banners.

The action should do more than look nice: it should make a demand clear. What problem does it show? What solution does it anticipate? Who should act afterwards?

## Design your street together

A street design workshop can help you and your neighbours turn everyday frustrations into a shared vision. Wir machen Wien uses a range of tools to explore problems and imagine alternatives. Choose the ones that fit your group and your goals.

1. **Choose a street or junction.** Pick a place your group knows and wants to improve.

2. **Gather the problems.** Look for heat, noise, narrow pavements, difficult crossings, parking pressure, conflicts, and a lack of seating or greenery.
   - **Street Shit Bingo:** Take a walk together to spot obstacles, hazards and surprising uses of street space.
   - **Leiwander Straßen Radar:** Use this simple survey sheet to explore quality of life, safety and where action is needed.

3. **Understand the space.** Measure the street or use plans to work out its dimensions.

4. **Build alternatives.** Imagine a range of changes: small improvements, bold ideas and longer-term possibilities.
   - **Street Design Table:** Adapt a street plan with paper, scissors and pens. Discuss different uses, how much space they need and what could change.
   - **Street Banner:** Show on site how the street’s width could be shared between walking, cycling, greenery and places to spend time.
   - **Tree Love Set:** Use mobile tree visualisations to show where shade and greenery are needed.

5. **Choose an immediate action.** Which temporary intervention could best bring your idea to life?

6. **Clarify permits and alliances.** Find out what permission you may need and who could support you.

7. **Document the action.** Gather photos, people’s views, observations of how the space is used, media coverage and political reactions. These can help you make the case for lasting change.
