# Component Showcase

This page is your Markdown reference. Markdown is the simple formatting language used to write all handbook chapters.

## Markdown basics

New to Markdown? Start with the [basic syntax overview on Markdown Guide](https://www.markdownguide.org/basic-syntax/) — it covers everything below in a few minutes.

Use `#` for headings. The more `#` characters, the lower the heading level. Use only one top-level heading (`#`) per page — that's the page title.

## Adding images

For examples, see the photos in Chapter 2:

```
<Figure src={"src/assets/book/berlin-kiezblock-bellermann.jpg"} alt={"Temporary closure and public space in the Bellermann neighborhood block in Berlin-Mitte."}>
Bellermann neighborhood block in Berlin-Mitte. Photo: BA SGA Mitte, L. Fritsche.
</Figure>
```

Manually change the filename in `src`—for example, from `src/assets/book/berlin-kiezblock-bellermann.jpg` to `src/assets/book/conversation-tool.png`.

Be sure to update the alt text too. It’s used by screen readers and displayed if the image doesn’t load. The text inside the `<Figure>` tag becomes the caption underneath the image.

You can also put several figures in a slideshow:

```
<Slideshow label="Sample images">
  <Figure src="lendplatz-graz-tactical-urbanism.jpg" alt="Lendplatz in Graz">Lendplatz in Graz.</Figure>
  <Figure src="galileigasse-tactical-urbanism.jpg" alt="Galileigasse in Vienna">Galileigasse in Vienna.</Figure>
</Slideshow>
```

Use <code>size="small"</code> or <code>size="medium"</code> to make a figure narrower without floating it. Add <code>float="left"</code> or <code>float="right"</code> when surrounding text should wrap around the image on wider screens; <code>float="center"</code> centers a narrower figure as a block.

Use <code>frame="white"</code> for logos or brand lockups that must stay on a white background in light and dark mode.

<div class="showcase-demo">
  <Figure src="erasmus-plus_en.png" alt="Co-funded by the European Union: Erasmus+" frame="white" size="small" />
</div>

<div class="showcase-demo">
  <Figure src="supergraetzel-favoriten-poller.jpg" alt="Bollards and planter rings in Supergrätzl Favoriten." size="small" float="right">
    Small floating figures can sit beside short explanatory text on wide screens.
  </Figure>
  <p>Use floating figures sparingly for side notes or examples. On narrow screens they return to the normal document flow so the image and text stay readable.</p>

<h2 class="showcase-label">MascotBox</h2>

A friendly Green Fix guide beside a speech bubble. Use it for a short explanation
or a practical nudge, not as a replacement for the chapter text. Animated mascots
play once whenever they enter or re-enter view, then hold the final frame. Hover or click the
mascot to replay once. Think stops at its raised-hand, yellow-rays idea pose rather
than returning to the questioning expression. Keyboard users can focus the
mascot and press Enter or Space.
Without JavaScript, with reduced motion,
and in browser print, PDF and EPUB, the matching still and all text remain visible.

```mdx
<MascotBox title="Try this first" pose="think" animated lang="en">
  Choose one street and ask your neighbours what they would change.
</MascotBox>
```

Choose `neutral` (or `idle`), `wave`, `point`, `celebrate`, `think`, `step`, or
`skeptical`. Omit `animated` for a still; all poses except `skeptical` have an
animated version. `treatment="linen"` selects the light-outline artwork on a dark
plate; the default is `ink`. Printed books always use Ink artwork for legibility.
Set `lang` to `en`, `de` or `sl` for the accessible replay label and title typography.

<div class="showcase-demo">
  <MascotBox title="I have an idea!" pose="think" animated lang="en">
    <p>Start with one small, visible improvement. What could you test on your street this month?</p>
  </MascotBox>
  <MascotBox title="Question the assumption" pose="skeptical">
    <p>Does everyone need to drive through this street, or just be able to reach it?</p>
  </MascotBox>
</div>
